Reasons from the rule

    Ease is fixed at 1. A higher score moves this card earlier in the queue; the number is not a probability.

    The difficult decision

    Recent misses raise priority, while recently shown cards receive a penalty. The rule must balance repeated practice with variety. A transparent score makes that tradeoff inspectable.

    Keep the trust boundary.

    A submitted result should agree with its answers and timing. The public helper exposes its checks and leaves the caller’s responsibilities explicit.

    Recompute correctness.

    The helper compares supplied selected and expected player IDs. The caller must provide a trusted expected answer; this function does not authenticate users or fetch an answer key.

    Result validation ↗

    Keep review flags distinct.

    High-risk results are rejected. Medium risk is accepted with a review flag. Calling every flag “rejected” would misrepresent the public example.

    Engineering decisions ↗

    Separate rules from the screen.

    Selected TypeScript modules can be run and tested independently. Decision records describe the practice, result-checking and same-team matching choices.
